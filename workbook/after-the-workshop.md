## Before you write

> [!WARNING]
> **Everything you post here is public**, and stays in the history of the repository even if deleted later.
>
> **Patterns, not names.** No client, no person, no case number, no court, no detail of an ongoing case. "A national portal refused a correction" is a pattern. A name, a date and a city are a case.

Answer only the questions you want. One sentence is enough. In English, or in your own language.

## Two ways to answer

| Way | For whom | How |
|-----|----------|-----|
| **Issue** | Everyone. No Git needed, only a GitHub account | Open the form: <https://github.com/caffe-doppio/dev-workshop/issues/new?template=after-the-workshop.yml> |
| **Pull request** | People who use Git | Create `workbook/answers/YYYYMMDD_your-github-name.md` from <https://github.com/caffe-doppio/dev-workshop/new/main/workbook/answers>, paste the questions below, answer, propose the change. GitHub makes the fork for you |

Answers are read, never edited. What we learn from them goes into the workbook, and the change is logged in the [changelog](../CHANGELOG.md).

## The questions

```markdown
Hub: AI Hub / Digital Democracy Hub / both / other

### To start
- What did you want to learn in this session? Did you get it?

### Evidence you collect
- What pieces do you collect for your strategic litigation today? Screenshots, emails, letters, exports, model outputs?
- Where do those pieces live right now? Email, a shared drive, a phone, a messaging app?
- Has a piece of digital evidence ever been challenged in one of your cases? On what ground?
- AI Hub: when you document an automated decision, a score, a risk rating, what exactly do you keep? The output, the input, the model version, the date?
- Digital Democracy Hub: when a portal or a platform says no, what do you have, apart from the person's word?
- How long after the event do you usually receive the evidence?

### The limits of technical knowledge
- Have you ever received evidence you could not read yourself? A log, an export, a file with a strange extension. What did you do?
- When you hit a technical wall, who do you call? Do you have that person?
- Have you ever had to explain something technical to a judge, or to a client? What worked? What did not?
- Has an expert ever said something in court that nobody in the room could check?
- Where is the line: what should a lawyer know, what can stay with the expert?
- Has a client ever asked you to look further than their own file?

### Looking back at the lab
- What surprised you?
- If you swapped roles: what was hardest?
- Which of the five tools would you actually use? Which one would never pass in your jurisdiction?
- What would a judge in your country need, to accept a HAR?
- What is the first thing you will do differently?
```
