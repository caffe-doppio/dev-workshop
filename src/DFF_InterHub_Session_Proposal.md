# Session Proposal — DFF AI & Digital Democracy InterHub Gathering
## Amsterdam, 7–8 October 2026

---

**"Borrowed from the Lab: Cybersecurity Methods for Evidence Management in Strategic Litigation"**

*Proposed format: participatory workshop — 90 minutes*

---

### Why this session

Across both hubs, organisations are building cases that rely on digital evidence — whether documenting discriminatory AI outputs or proving patterns of administrative exclusion and online censorship. Yet most litigators lack access to the evidence management protocols that cybersecurity professionals use as standard practice. The result: evidence gathered in good faith may be inadmissible, improperly shared, or inadequately authenticated by the time it reaches a court.

This workshop proposes to bridge that gap by introducing selected cybersecurity tools and methodologies, reframed specifically for the legal and litigation context.

---

### What we would cover

- **Traffic Light Protocol (TLP)** as a classification framework for evidence sharing during case preparation — controlling who can access what, and when, across legal teams and coalition partners
- **Cryptographic hashing and timestamping** (SHA-256 / OpenTimestamps): how to prove the existence and integrity of a piece of evidence without necessarily disclosing its content at the coalition level
- **Chain of custody** adapted to European legal standards and digital-native documents
- A brief introduction to responsible OSINT techniques for documenting institutional or platform-level conduct

Each method is framed around three questions: *what problem does it solve / how do you apply it / what does admissibility look like before a judge with limited digital literacy.*

---

### InterHub connection

The session is designed to be equally relevant to both hubs. AI Hub participants documenting algorithmic harm and Digital Democracy Hub participants documenting administrative or platform-based exclusion face the same foundational challenge: knowing what to preserve, how to authenticate it, who may access it, and how to present it to a court. Mixed-hub working groups during the practical exercise are a feature, not an afterthought — the scenario-based exercise is specifically designed to surface how case types differ and where protocols converge.

---

### Format

| Block | Content | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Framing the evidence gap — illustrated with case types from each hub | 15 min |
| 2 | Tool walkthrough — participatory, hands-on | 40 min |
| 3 | Scenario exercise in mixed-hub groups | 25 min |
| 4 | Shared takeaways and resource-building | 10 min |
